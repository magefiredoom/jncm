## 1. Summary
A solo research project (undergraduate, one cluster allocation) building and comparing hierarchical, coarse-to-fine text generators on TinyStories: NCM's discrete VQ codes, a continuous capacity-bounded variant, and a JEPA-style joint training mode. The codebase (package `jncm`, C-free PyTorch) was written by coding agents from a written spec (Gemini draft, then OpenCode/DeepSeek and Buffy/Freebuff). Tokenizers are trained and pass their gate; generator and JEPA runs on the cluster are the next step. A paper draft exists as a Claude Doc.

## 2. Goal, claims, deadlines
- Question: does replacing NCM's discrete codes with continuous representations, optionally trained JEPA-style, change generation sample efficiency at a matched information budget?
- Claims for paper 1 (4-page ICLR 2027 workshop paper): C1 controlled discrete-vs-continuous comparison at nominally matched bits per level; C2 a capacity-bounded continuous hierarchy; C3 stochastic versus deterministic per-level heads, with diversity metrics that expose the deterministic head's collapse at the coarsest level; C4 JEPA-style joint training with teacher-forced EMA targets plus a fair accounting rule. A negative JEPA result is acceptable and is planned for ("honest ablations including at least one informative failure").
- Paper 2 (later): adaptive verifier re-looping on top of this system.
- Venue: ICLR 2027 workshops. ICLR lists Feb 1, 2027 as the suggested workshop-contribution deadline; each workshop sets its own; workshop acceptances are announced Nov 29, 2026, so pick the workshop after that [verify dates on iclr.cc].
- Fallback: if the JEPA variant does not match the best staged variant (gate G2), the paper becomes a discrete-vs-continuous study with JEPA as a reported negative result. The original "pivot to a hybrid" kill criterion is superseded by the gates.

## 3. Design decisions and why (docs/SPEC.md is authoritative; it was v1.5 at handoff)
- **Hierarchy.** L=128 BPE tokens (vocab 8192), K=12 levels, l_k = 1 + (2(k-1)(L-1) + (K-1)) // (2(K-1)) = [1,13,24,36,47,59,70,82,93,105,116,128] (exact half-up rounding, not Python's banker's rounding). Residual chain zhat_k = zhat_{k-1} + R_k(code_k) with area pooling down and linear interpolation plus depthwise conv up. Nested dropout (p=0.5, one cutoff per batch) in tokenizer training only. One-block bidirectional decoder.
- **vq tokenizer.** 4096 codes of width 64 per level, EMA codebooks (decay 0.99), dead-code reinit, commitment 0.25, straight-through, all arithmetic in fp32 (bf16 crashed `index_add_` on GPU). `Encoded.codes` are straight-through vectors; `targets` are integer indices; the generator conditions from `targets`.
- **noisy (continuous) tokenizer.** Code = LayerNorm(no affine)(W pool(r)) with d_k=8; the chain uses code + sigma*eps (sigma=0.4) in train AND eval; generator targets are the clean codes. Capacity bound (d_k/2)log2(1+1/sigma^2) = 11.43 bits versus 12 for vq. Why: an unbounded continuous code lets the hierarchy collapse (everything in level 1), so we bound information with a power constraint plus a Gaussian channel. The match is nominal; see section 5.
- **Generator.** 12 layers, H=768 (lite: 8 layers, H=512) with attention restricted within each level (block diagonal, levels padded to 128). Input to level k = cumulative reconstruction of levels < k pooled to l_k positions, plus normalized sinusoidal positions and a level embedding. Level 1 sees zeros. All levels train in one pass; sampling is exactly K backbone passes. The input embedding belongs to the generator and must receive gradients (review finding F11).
- **Heads.** `ce` (for vq), `flow` (per-level conditional flow-matching MLP on clean codes, 8 Euler steps, 4 draws per position), `regress` (MSE to the clean code, layer-normed at sampling, channel noise added). Continuous heads add fresh channel noise at sampling. `regress` is deterministic, so with level 1 having one position and zero conditioning it can only emit one level-1 code: samples collapse. This is expected and is part of the story (Q8); tests accept it and `unique_frac` / `pairwise_agree` measure it.
- **Corruption.** Swap conditioning codes within their level at rates decreasing linearly 0.8 to 0.2 across levels; targets stay clean. These numbers are my guess (NCM reports moderate corruption matters); a small sweep is planned (review F16).
- **JEPA mode.** Online encoder E, EMA copy (momentum 0.996 to 1 cosine, kept in eval mode, updated after `optimizer.step`), decoder D, generator G, trained jointly: L = L_dec + L_pred + L_vic. Targets are teacher-forced on the online chain by default (`jepa.target_chain: online`): t_k = LayerNorm(Ebar.proj_k(pool(zbar - zhat_{k-1}.detach()))). Reason: the online decoder is trained on a noisy chain whose finer levels correct coarser noise; clean-chain targets (`ema_clean`, kept as an ablation) cannot. Property test: with the EMA copy equal to the online parameters, `online` targets equal staged `enc.targets` for the same noise draw. VICReg (gamma=1, lambda_var=1.0, lambda_cov=0.04) on online clean codes and on predictions. Flags: `ema`, `vicreg`, `decoder_through_E`, `head` (regress or flow; `ce` is rejected), `target_chain`. Default head for JEPA: flow.
- **Accounting (SPEC 6.2).** Staged `total_positions` = tokenizer positions (655M for 10k steps x 512 x 128) + generator positions. Joint runs record `joint_positions` = steps x B x L, `total_positions` = that, and `total_positions_conservative` = 2x that. Plots and `gate_report.py` use both. Milestone checkpoints every 10% for every run.

## 4. Evaluation protocol and reference numbers
- Generative perplexity under GPT-2-large after decoding to text and re-tokenizing (first token unscored, no truncation), mean per-sample unigram entropy, plus diversity: `unique_frac` (distinct samples) and `pairwise_agree` (mean fraction of identical positions over random pairs). 256 samples x 5 seeds for dev.
- Our pipeline on real validation text: Gen PPL 10.80 (+/-0.26), H 4.03. NCM reports 9.67 and 3.96 for its pipeline, and 15.41 / 3.98 for its model, a ratio of about 1.59 (so about 17 would be comparable here). Always report ratios to our own data row; absolute numbers are not comparable to NCM's.
- Gates (docs/TASKS.md): G0a tokenizers (vq >= 0.999, noisy >= 0.995 exact-token accuracy), passed. G0: vq + ce Gen PPL <= 20 with H >= 3.8. G1: noisy + flow within 15% of G0 at equal `total_positions`, H within 0.15 of 4.03; noisy + regress reported only (flag `pairwise_agree` > 0.5). G2: JEPA matches or beats the best staged run at equal positions under both accountings. `scripts/gate_report.py` compares only inside the observed range and prints "not comparable" otherwise.

## 5. Results so far (G0a, 10,000 steps, batch 512, about 2.3 H100-hours each)
- vq (job 2880034, 2:21:20): acc_full 0.99998. noisy (job 2880035, 2:16:49): acc_full 0.99994 at a nominal 11.43 bits. One fixed validation batch of 512 windows (65,536 tokens), so differences at the 1e-4 level are not significant.
- acc_cum by level, vq: 0.078, 0.144, 0.291, 0.614, 0.753, 0.885, 0.937, 0.972, 0.986, 0.993, 0.9945, 1.000. noisy: 0.078, 0.108, 0.218, 0.411, 0.616, 0.780, 0.888, 0.950, 0.978, 0.990, 0.995, 0.9999. vq is more front-loaded (level 4: 0.61 vs 0.41); the continuous code realizes less of its capacity bound at intermediate levels. Hypothesis for G1, not a conclusion: more information in vq's coarse levels makes its conditioning stronger but its first levels harder to generate.
- Noisy diagnostics show no collapse: mean per-dim std 0.91 at level 1 and at least 0.99 elsewhere, effective rank 6.2 at level 1 and 6.8 to 7.0 elsewhere (max 7). vq codebook usage is 73% to 99% at levels 2 to 10, then 79% and 57% at the two finest levels (level-1 usage is capped by the 512-sample batch and is not informative).
- If noisy+flow lags G0 by more than 15%, the cheap levers are a larger d_k or a smaller sigma. Either breaks the nominal matched-bits claim, so that is a researcher decision, not an agent decision.
- Pilot: `pilot_vq` (500 steps) took 9:05 including startup. Tokenizer steps cost about 0.82 to 0.85 s, which looks overhead-bound for a 36M-parameter model; a timing diagnosis (data wait fraction, prefetch, compile) was proposed and not confirmed as done.
